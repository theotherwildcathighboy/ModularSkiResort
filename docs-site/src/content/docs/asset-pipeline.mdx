## Generation

Procedural generators in Blender produce every mesh, LOD chain and texture set in the mod.

- **Source:** the generator is the asset. A change is a rebuild, never a hand edit of an exported file.

## Budgets

Triangle and texture budgets per asset class, taken from comparable vanilla assets of the same footprint and role, then set by judgment.

- **Caps:** a cap is a ceiling. Detail beyond it goes into the texture.
- **Textures:** **512²** minimum per map, any kind.

## Texturing

Repeating swatches up close, small per-asset maps at distance.

- **Tile sheet:** one shared **4096²** sheet of material swatches per building family at LOD0. Faces are cut to swatch size so repeats come from reuse, as in vanilla.
- **Far maps:** each asset carries its own maps for LOD1 and LOD2.
- **Own atlas:** the Ski Bus Depot is off the family sheets. It bakes one **2048²** atlas from real-world-scale scans tinted to its palette, at about **100** px/m on its main surfaces. A unique bake at this size cannot reach the wall band.
- **Texel density:** held in a band per surface class. Target for building walls: the vanilla band of **340** to **520** px/m. Large station surfaces: **120** to **200** px/m. Roof-edge trim: strip-mapped along its length at **400** px/m across, at no triangle cost.
- **Material split:** the vanilla set. Base color, normal, mask, control mask (recolor channels), emissive.
- **Fine detail:** features under **5 cm** live in the normal map and span at least **3** texels. Weathering is low frequency and opaque.

## Detail Allocation

Triangles go where a player reads them at play distance; texture and normal map carry the rest.

- **Spent on:** entrances (recessed doors with casing, threshold and hardware on every leaf), glazing (glass set back in a real opening with casings and sills), roof edges, chimneys and flues, lift machinery (sheave trains and wheels).
- **Held back on:** large plain walls (few quads on a swatch), undersides, small relief (seams, bolts and grain in the normal map), hidden faces, far LODs.
- **Structure:** every fitting touches its support within **5 mm**; nothing crosses glass.

## Vanilla First

Where the game has a prop, fixture or detail, the asset uses it.

- **Method:** the game's own sub-objects, referenced by GUID: rooftop units, vents, wall lights and lanterns, benches, bins, racks, fences, poles and trees.
- **Benefit:** vanilla detail, consistent with the rest of the city, at **0** triangles against the asset's cap.
- **Modeled in-house:** only what the game lacks, such as chimney crowns, string lights, patrol beacons and lift machinery.
- **Ski Bus Depot:** the shelter, sign tower and corten planter are modeled. The bench, bin, lamp, trees and rocks are vanilla.
- **Ski bus coach:** the game's own bus meshes, with **0** new triangles. The SKI BUS livery is a decal on the sides and roof, so it reads as paint rather than a bolted-on sign. At night the lit windows show through the side letters; the roof stays readable.

## Recolor Channels

Three recolor channels per building, through the game's color picker.

- **Channels:** red, green and blue of the control mask = slots **1**, **2** and **3**.
- **Palette:** one palette per building. Every part takes its color from the building's own set.

## Review Loop

Every asset passes the same gates from preview to play.

1. **Preview:** rendered previews, including LOD and recolor views, reviewed before export. No export without approval.
2. **Import:** through the game's Editor, one action per texture-sharing group.
3. **Prefab wiring:** the gameplay prefab is pointed at the new mesh and given its color, LOD and emissive properties.
4. **Automated checks:** triangle and LOD budgets, LOD ratios, texel density, texture names and sizes, prefab validity, vanilla references.
5. **Field test:** the asset placed and played in a city, day and night, before it ships.
