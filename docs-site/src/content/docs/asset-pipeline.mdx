For modelers. Every asset in the mod is generated by script in headless Blender, previewed, approved, exported, imported in the Editor and wired by prefab edits. Numbers below are the current shipped constants.

<Aside type="note" title="Scope">
Tools used: Blender (headless Python generators), the Cities: Skylines II Editor (import), a text editor for prefab JSON. No asset is hand-modeled; a change is a change to the generator, then a rebuild.
</Aside>

## Triangle Budgets

Triangle (tri) cap per asset at LOD0. **A cap is a ceiling.**

- **Yardstick:** vanilla class. A vanilla crane tower is 2,016 tris.
- **Rule:** the gap between a building's target and its cap is the fudge. Detail beyond the cap is done in the texture.
- **Enforcement:** the cap is a validator error, checked at the weathering level the building ships with. A build that fits only by dropping a weathering level is over.
- **Exception:** the Grand Lodge's clock numbers, hands and louvres keep full detail, over the cap by a measured margin.

### How Caps Were Set

- **Lodges and Ski School:** one cap per building, fixed when the tile sheet landed. Tiling raised LOD0 from 6,763 to 10,036 tris on the A-Frame Day Lodge and from 23,019 to 29,916 on the Grand Lodge. Caps stayed put, so the gap between target and cap is the margin.
- **Grand Lodge:** 33,500 = measured 31,812 × 1.05, rounded up. No other cap moved.
- **Towers:** cap = the larger of the original cap and the smallest multiple of 100 giving at least 5 % headroom.
- **Wheels:** a tower carries 8 to 12 wheels. At 224 tris each, a 25-tower line spent 67,200 tris on wheels alone. One wheel at 120 tris or fewer cut that to 24,000 (fixed-grip), 39,200 (detachable) and 49,000 (gondola) per 25-tower line.

## LODs

Cheaper copies of the mesh for distance. The importer generates none; each level is exported as its own FBX.

- **LOD1:** at most **0.50 × LOD0**. A 0.52 target failed the validator. Lift exports measure at 0.464 × LOD0 or less.
- **LOD2:** at most **500** tris on buildings.
- **Build order:** delete sub-pixel parts by name prefix, then decimate to an absolute target.
- **Wheels:** never decimated. LOD1 is an 8-segment band (28 tris); LOD2 has no wheel.
- **Window holes:** LOD0 only.
- **Far maps:** LOD1 and LOD2 carry their own 1024² maps (Grand Lodge 2048²). Tram stations ship far sets at 2048 and 1024.
- **Engine base:** a ground part has no bottom face and nothing below 0; otherwise the engine draws no foundation.

## Texel Density

Texels per meter (px/m): texture pixels covering one meter of model. The quality number.

- **Vanilla, small buildings:** 346 (houses) to 518 (EU hotel).
- **Vanilla, large buildings:** 93 (hospital) to 304 (towers); TrainStation02 257.
- **Before the tile sheet:** 10 to 46 px/m. Reviewers called the stone blurry. The cause was density; compression was ruled out.
- **Now:** walls and roofs 300 to 400 px/m on the tile sheet. Grand Lodge 171 to 200; tram stations 120 to 200 on big surfaces.
- **Texture floor:** 512² per map, any kind.
- **Mip margin:** islands keep a margin of max(16, atlas ÷ 64) px so distant mips do not bleed.
- **Alpha:** any BaseColor alpha below 255 turns the whole surface into cutout. Weathering uses opaque swatches only.
- **Fine detail:** features under 5 cm go in the normal map and must span at least 3 texels. Weathering overlays are at least 5 cm and 8 texels, low frequency only.

### Tile Sheet

A shared 4096² sheet of repeating material swatches, 8 × 8 cells of 512 px.

- **Why:** the Editor binds a texture to a model only by file-name suffix, and only 16 suffixes exist. Per-material surfaces are impossible; vanilla does not use them either. Vanilla paints one big sheet and reuses it (the EU hotel reuses each texel about 37×).
- **Repetition:** vanilla stores nearly all maps as Clamp (3,657 of 3,696), so repeats come from cutting faces to swatch size and stacking identical islands. Towers reuse each texel 45 to 295×.
- **Family sheet:** all eight buildings use one sheet at LOD0. The Ski School owns it; the others share it through `settings.json`.
- **Import:** the sheet owner and every sharer are imported in **one Editor action**. Sharers imported later get a byte-identical copy (about 107 MB for five lodges).
- **Memory:** building texture memory fell from 344.2 MB to 173.4 MB.
- **Price:** tile cuts cost tris. A 10 × 4 m wall is 2 tris as one quad and about 16 cut into 3.2 m tiles. A blanket cut added 30 to 48 % to lodge tri counts.
- **Strip UV:** long trim (ridge, rake, fascia) maps once along its length on a grain swatch, 400 px/m across. 0 extra tris.

### Tram Sheet

Tram stations use their own 4096² sheet; the base owns it, the top shares it.

- **Steered cuts:** swatch sizes follow the station grid (6 m column pitch, 4.8 m stone story, 1.2 m deck plates). Cuts go only on big faces; steel and rails are strip-mapped; repeated parts are stacked.
- **Cost:** +936 tris on the base (+6 %) and +556 on the top (+4 %), against +51 % and +39 % for a blanket cut.
- **Glass:** plain `_Gls` clear glass. This freed 348 tris on the base.
- **Before:** one unique atlas gave 22.5 px/m over 9,217 m².

## Vanilla First

Rule (set by the author): if a sub-object, fixture or detail exists in the game, reference it instead of modeling it.

- **Why:** vanilla props carry more detail and add 0 tris to the asset's cap.
- **Method:** a sub-object row names the prop by verified GUID. GUIDs are read from the game's `resources.assets` prefab map, and preflight whitelists them.
- **Rooftop units:** `ACMachineRoofRandom01`, every variant, 1.0 to 1.5 m, 228 to 346 tris each. No curb, no gap. On a sloped roof: the heat-pump variant on the wall, low.
- **Vents:** `ACVentRoofRandom01` for ridge vents.
- **Lights:** vanilla wall lights for lanterns and sign lamps.
- **Also vanilla:** trees, benches, bins, tables, chairs, lamp posts, planters, bollards, fences, fire pits.
- **Mismatched variants:** a placeholder whose variants differ in style (`WallLightRandom01`) does not fit a matched pair. Mimic one style instead, with the vanilla reference rendered beside it.
- **Snow:** vanilla parasols and deck chairs vanish while the owner's snow value is 15 or more (accepted).
- **Terrain:** a row follows the terrain only with parent mesh `-1`; `0` is rigid on the building.

### Modeled In-House

Only where a search of the vanilla prop families finds nothing.

- **Parts:** stovepipes, flue pots, chimney crowns, string lights (as of 2026-10-19).
- **Dressing kit:** 8 parts in 11 meshes on one shared 1024² set at 255 px/m, 1,936 tris for one of each. Ski gear is one bindingless part: ski pair 32 tris, board 28.
- **Stand-ins:** review packs draw every vanilla row as a labeled semi-transparent box, so placement is judged before import.

## Detail Spending

Spend triangles where the eye reads them at play distance; hold them back where a texture or the normal map does the job.

### Spent

- **Windows:** glass 10 cm behind the wall in a real hole, 6 cm casings, sill with drip. 54 tris per standard sash; about 7 to 9 per hole. Vanilla glass sits 7.7 to 15.5 cm back.
- **Doors:** leaf 8 to 10 cm back in a hole with casing and threshold. 28 to 52 tris single, 70 to 86 double. Every leaf carries a pull, lever or knob (6 to 18 tris); the generator adds one to a door without one.
- **Roof ridge:** a 12-tri bent flashing; trim strips on a grain swatch cost 0 tris.
- **Chimneys and fittings:** crowns with a 6 cm drip, pots with flashing and collar, gutters, downpipes, snow guards.
- **Tram halls:** the interior is a visible class and gets exterior densities, since the hall can be seen through clear glass.
- **Wheels:** one wheel family, 76 tris plus 22 per visible hub boss.

### Held Back

- **Flat walls:** large faces stay as few quads on a swatch; no extra cuts.
- **Bevels:** vanilla paints bevels in the normal map. A beveled box costs about 44 tris against 12.
- **Small relief:** seams, bolt heads and grain under 5 cm go in the normal map.
- **Hidden hubs:** a boss on a wheel face that is not visible is culled.
- **Weathering:** the Grand Lodge ships weathering level 4 (the least) to stay under its cap.
- **Glass interiors:** vanilla's `_Win` fake interiors read wrong in a ski building. All eight buildings use plain `_Gls` with lit backing plates.

## Process

Claude generates; the author reviews previews, imports in the Editor and tests in the game.

<Steps>

1. **Reference:** photos first. Fix the recolor slots (R, G, B channels of the control mask = slots 1, 2, 3). One palette per building; deviations need a stated story.
2. **Generate:** headless Blender script builds the asset, its LODs and maps. Each export gets a new name so the game never confuses two versions.
3. **Preview:** renders and a review pack, judged in a live Blender. No export before the verdict.
4. **Export:** FBX, `_BaseColor`, `_Normal`, `_MaskMap`, `_ControlMask` and `settings.json` in its own folder for its category. The game negates FBX x on import; sub-object rows carry that mirror.
5. **Import:** Editor, No Preset. Texture-sharing groups go in as one action.
6. **Surgery:** repoint the hand-made prefab to the new mesh ids, add `ColorProperties`, `LodProperties` and `EmissiveProperties` as needed, validate, mirror to the repo.
7. **Test:** full game restart, error popups counted first, then the feature.

</Steps>

## Gates

A check that fails the build on a known mistake. Each lesson from a field test becomes a gate.

- **Asset check:** texture names, UV, alpha below 255, LOD1 above 0.50 × LOD0, LOD0 cap, LOD2 cap, engine-base ring, texture floor 512², per-part density, FBX round trip.
- **Preflight:** whole-project check of prefabs, ids, rope lanes, spawn markers, vanilla GUID whitelist. Expected: 0 FAIL, 0 WARN.
- **Prefab validation:** every edited prefab.
- **Blender tests:** build determinism (two builds hash identically), flag-off byte-identical, triangle and LOD budgets, wheel family, door hardware.
- **Structure audits:** nothing floats (every fitting touches its support within 5 mm), nothing crosses glass, one sheave wheel on every tower and station.
